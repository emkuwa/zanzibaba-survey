The main factors that affect survey cost

1. Type of survey

A topographical survey, boundary verification, construction setting out, plot subdivision and engineering survey solve different problems and require different field work and outputs.

2. Plot size and site conditions

Larger areas generally require more field coverage. Dense vegetation, difficult access, steep terrain, existing buildings or many physical features may also affect the amount of work required.

3. Required detail and deliverables

An architect may require site levels, contours and existing features, while an engineer may specify control, setting-out points, levels or as-built information. The quotation should reflect the actual brief rather than a generic survey package.

4. Location and access

The site location in Zanzibar, travel requirements and practical access to the plot can affect mobilisation and field time.

Topographical survey cost

For a topographical survey in Zanzibar, the useful starting information is the Google Maps pin, approximate site area and intended development. If your architect or engineer has provided a survey brief, send it with the inquiry.

This allows the survey team to determine the level of detail required instead of quoting a misleading one-size-fits-all rate.

Boundary verification cost

For boundary verification, available land and survey documents matter. Send photos or copies of the documents you have together with the location and reason for the boundary check.

Plot subdivision cost

Plot subdivision depends on the existing land information, total area, intended number or sizes of new plots and applicable requirements. It should not be priced simply by assuming a fixed fee per plot.

Construction and engineering survey cost

For construction setting out or an engineering survey, send the site location and relevant drawings. The construction stage and points or outputs required by the contractor, engineer or consultant help define the scope.

How to get an accurate survey quotation

For most inquiries, send these details:

1. Google Maps pin or plot location
2. Approximate plot size or dimensions
3. Type of project or reason for the survey
4. Available land documents or drawings
5. Survey brief from your architect, engineer or consultant, if available
