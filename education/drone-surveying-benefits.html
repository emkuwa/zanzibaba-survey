Drone Surveying: Uses and Limitations

Aerial imagery can support mapping, site documentation and measurement on suitable projects, but the method and expected accuracy must be matched to the required deliverable.

Where drone surveying can help

Drone data can be useful for larger sites, development documentation, progress records, terrain mapping and other assignments where an aerial perspective or dense imagery adds value.

Possible outputs

Depending on the equipment, processing method, control and agreed scope, outputs may include georeferenced imagery, orthomosaics, surface models, point-cloud information, contours or other mapping products.

Accuracy is project-specific

There is no responsible single accuracy promise for every drone survey. Results depend on factors such as equipment, flight planning, ground control/check points, terrain, vegetation, image quality, processing and the required coordinate/reference system.

Drone mapping does not automatically replace ground surveying. Some projects require ground control, detail that is not visible from the air, boundary/land-record work or conventional survey observations.

Permissions, weather and site conditions

Flight feasibility can depend on location, airspace or operational requirements, weather, site access and safety. These factors should be reviewed for the specific assignment before scheduling.
